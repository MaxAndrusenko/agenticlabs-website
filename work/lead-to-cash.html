Sales Cloud Integration Agentforce

Rebuilding a broken lead-to-cash pipeline for a Series C SaaS company.

How a senior architect redesigned the data model, rebuilt the automation, and wired billing back together — with every line human-reviewed.

The problem

A fast-growing Series C SaaS company had outgrown a lead-to-cash process that had been stitched together one workaround at a time. Lead capture was disconnected — marketing forms, partner referrals, and inbound sign-ups landed in different objects with no shared ownership rules, so reps chased duplicates while genuinely new demand sat untouched. Routing depended on a chain of point-and-click automations that nobody fully understood and no one wanted to change.

Downstream, the cracks widened. Pricing and approvals ran through a manual CPQ process kept alive in spreadsheets, so every non-standard quote became a bottleneck. The handoff into billing was worse: opportunities that closed in Salesforce were re-keyed by hand into the billing and ERP systems, which meant delayed invoices, revenue-recognition mismatches, and a finance team that no longer trusted the numbers in the CRM. Quote-to-cash had quietly become the company's slowest, least predictable process.

Our approach

We started where the failures actually originated: the data model. A senior architect redesigned how leads, accounts, opportunities, quotes, and orders relate — establishing clear ownership, deduplication keys, and a single source of truth before any automation was touched. That design became the contract every later decision was measured against.

From there we rebuilt the automation deliberately. Deterministic, high-volume logic — routing, approvals, order creation — moved into Apex where it could be tested and reasoned about; genuinely declarative steps stayed in Flow so the client's admins could own them. The billing and ERP handoff was re-implemented as an event-driven integration over platform events, so a closed opportunity reliably produces an invoice without anyone re-typing a record.

For lead triage, we scoped an Agentforce agent to the parts of the process that genuinely benefit from judgment at scale — enriching, classifying, and prioritizing inbound leads against the new data model — with hard guardrails and a human in the loop on anything ambiguous. It accelerates the queue; it does not silently decide who a rep never calls.

AI accelerated the mechanical work throughout — scaffolding classes, drafting tests, generating first-pass mappings — but every line that reached the org was read, understood, and approved by a senior engineer. The AI did the typing. Judgment stayed human.

The result

The rebuilt pipeline moves a lead from capture to invoice as one continuous, observable process instead of a series of manual handoffs. Finance and revenue operations now work from the same numbers, quotes clear faster, and the automation is documented and owned rather than feared. Specifically, the engagement delivered:

  • A single, documented data model spanning lead capture through order and billing.
  • An event-driven integration to the billing and ERP systems that eliminated manual re-keying.
  • A scoped Agentforce triage agent with guardrails and human review on every escalation.
  • Full source control, test coverage, and staged deployment across the rebuild.
  • A knowledge-transfer handoff so the in-house team owns and extends what we built.

The problem

Twelve years of customization, no clean seam to cut on.

The org had accumulated more than a decade of point-in-time customization — overlapping objects, fields that meant different things to different teams, and automation layered on automation. The underlying data model no longer matched how the business actually ran.

Duplicate records were widespread, with no single reliable key to match on. Worse, a live Experience Cloud portal sat on top of the same data, serving real customers every day. Any migration had to preserve portal access, identity, and history without a maintenance window that would take the portal offline.

  • A tangled legacy data model spread across 18 objects, several of them redundant.
  • Duplicate customer records with no dependable natural or external key.
  • A production Experience Cloud portal that had to stay live and consistent throughout.
  • Automation and validation rules that would fire unpredictably during a bulk load.
The approach

Architecture first, then staged migration with reconciliation on every batch.

We started with an assessment of the existing model before moving a single record, then designed the target schema and a migration pipeline that could be rehearsed, reconciled, and reversed. AI accelerated the mechanical work — field mapping, transform scripts, dedupe heuristics — while a senior engineer signed off on every mapping and validated every batch.

01

Architecture-first assessment

Mapped the legacy model, the portal's dependencies, and the target schema before writing any migration code.

02

Dedupe & AI-accelerated mapping

AI drafted mapping and transform scripts and surfaced duplicate clusters; a senior engineer reviewed and corrected every rule.

03

Sandbox rehearsals

Ran the full pipeline repeatedly in sandbox to catch automation side effects and tune batch sizing before touching production.

04

Staged load with reconciliation

Migrated in staged batches, reconciling counts and field-level values against the source and validating every batch by hand.

The result

A clean migration onto a modern model, with the portal never going dark.

Every batch reconciled. No customer records were lost, and duplicates were resolved into clean, canonical records on the new schema. The Experience Cloud portal stayed live through the cutover, with customer identity and history intact.

The business came out of the migration with a data model that matches how it actually operates — simpler to report on, safer to automate against, and ready for the next phase of work rather than fighting a decade of accumulated debt.

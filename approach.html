Our approach

Most engagements ask for trust up front. We’d rather earn it.

Long contracts, opaque scoping, junior developers billed at senior rates, and an org only your vendor understands — these are the reasons Salesforce projects go wrong, and they all trace back to the same thing: commercial structures that make it expensive to leave.

We built our approach on the opposite assumption. If leaving is cheap and easy, we have to be worth keeping every week. That’s the whole deal.

How a week works
  • Scope and price for the week are agreed in advance, in writing.
  • We demo what shipped on Friday.
  • The invoice follows the demo — we bill for delivered work, not planned work.
  • If a week disappoints, you stop. You’ve risked one week.

After the trust period — usually four to eight weeks — you choose what happens next. We’ll recommend one. You decide.

Easy exit

Leaving should cost you nothing but the notice

  • Every commit lands in your repository, from day one — never ours.
  • All configuration deployed through your pipeline, into your org.
  • Documentation written as we go, not assembled during offboarding.
  • No proprietary frameworks, no unmanaged black boxes, no license traps.
  • One week’s written notice. No termination fee, no minimum term, no clawback.
  • Structured handover: knowledge-transfer session, runbook, and an open-items register for whoever picks it up.

We’d rather lose a client cleanly and be recommended, than keep one who wants to leave. Every guarantee above is written into the MSA, not just this page — ask for the clause numbers and we’ll send them before you sign.
